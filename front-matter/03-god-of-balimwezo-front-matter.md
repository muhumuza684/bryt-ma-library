# VOLUME ONE
## GOD OF BALIMWEZO
### How One Man's Obedience Opened Another Man's Door

### FRONT COVER
**GOD OF BALIMWEZO**
*How One Man's Obedience Opened Another Man's Door*

### BACK COVER COPY
Ronald Balimwezo did not set out to become the Lord Mayor of Kampala. He set out to serve — as an engineer, a lecturer, a local councillor, one division at a time, for years, without applause. But the door that finally let his service reach an entire city was not one he built himself.

This is a book about that door — who opened it, why, and what it means when God chooses to lift one person's life through a decision someone else made, for entirely different reasons of their own.

### INTRODUCTION
Every calling looks small from the inside. Ronald Balimwezo spent years doing the unglamorous work of local government — an engineer by training, a councillor by service, faithful to a single division of Kampala long before anyone outside that division knew his name. There was nothing in that season of his life that looked like preparation for citywide leadership. It looked, simply, like showing up.

At the same time, in a completely different corner of Uganda's story, a musician was making a decision that had nothing to do with Ronald Balimwezo at all. He was deciding whether to leave behind a career built on music and step into a fight he had never trained for — politics. That decision was costly, uncertain, and by every reasonable measure, none of his business to make on anyone else's behalf.

And yet.

Years later, the door that decision opened is the same door Ronald Balimwezo walked through to become Lord Mayor of Kampala.

This book is not a political biography. It is not an argument for any party or position. It is a meditation on a much older, much simpler truth: that God often moves people through doors they never asked to walk through, using the obedience of one person to prepare an opportunity for someone else entirely — an opportunity neither of them could have seen coming, and neither of them built alone.

If you have ever wondered whether your faithfulness in a small, unnoticed place is being wasted — this book is for you. And if you have ever wondered whether someone else's brave decision, made for reasons that had nothing to do with you, quietly became the door through which your own life changed — this book is for you too.

### APPENDIX — Reflection Questions
1. What door in your own life did you walk through that someone else opened, without either of you realizing it at the time?
2. Where in your life are you being faithful in a small place, not yet knowing what it's preparing you for?

# VOLUME TWO
## THE DOOR BOBI WINE OPENED

### FRONT COVER
**THE DOOR BOBI WINE OPENED**

*A story about the people who walked through it.*

### BACK COVER COPY
When a musician turns politician, the story is usually told about the musician. This book tells it differently — through the people who walked through the door his decision opened. An engineer who became a mayor. A journalist who became the nation's Leader of Opposition. Neither of them set out to be politicians. Both of them found themselves standing in a doorway that didn't exist before one man decided to build it.

### INTRODUCTION
This is not really a book about Bobi Wine. It only starts with him, the way a hallway starts with a door.

In 2017, a well-known Ugandan musician made the decision to contest a parliamentary seat — a decision that, on its own, changed exactly one man's life. But decisions like that rarely stay contained to the person who makes them. Within a few years, that single choice had become a party, a movement, and a platform — and through that platform, people who had never planned on a life in politics found themselves standing somewhere they never expected to be.

Ronald Balimwezo, an engineer and local government veteran, became Lord Mayor of Kampala. Joel Ssenyonyi, a journalist who once worked behind a microphone rather than in front of a podium, became the country's Leader of Opposition. Neither man is the story here on his own. Together, they are proof of something larger: that one person's decision to walk through a door can, without anyone planning it, become the doorway for several other people's lives.

This book follows both of their paths — not as political endorsement, and not as commentary on any side of Uganda's politics, but as a story about how doors work. Someone has to build them. Most of the people who walk through them never met the builder, and never asked him to build it for them. That is, in a sense, the whole point.

### APPENDIX — A Note on Sources
The events described in this book are drawn from public record — election results, official appointments, and public statements made by the individuals involved. Where personal reflections or motivations are described, they are drawn from statements the individuals have made publicly, not speculated upon.

# VOLUME THREE
## KINGMAKER
### What Bobi Wine's Politics Reveals About How God Lifts People

### FRONT COVER
**KINGMAKER**
*What Bobi Wine's Politics Reveals About How God Lifts People*

### BACK COVER COPY
An engineer. A singer. A comedian. None of them set out to lead. All three ended up in office — a mayor's seat, a divisional mayorship, a seat in parliament — through the same platform, built by a man who was once just a musician with a following.

*Kingmaker* studies the pattern underneath these three stories, and asks a bigger question than politics can answer on its own: what does it mean when the same door keeps opening for different people, in different ways, at different times — and none of them built the door themselves?

### INTRODUCTION
There is a pattern here that is easy to miss if you only look at one story at a time.

An engineer named Ronald Balimwezo becomes Lord Mayor of Kampala. A singer named Ali Bukeni — known to most as Nubian Li — becomes Mayor of Nakawa Division, years after he was the one who encouraged a hesitant friend to enter politics in the first place. A comedian named Hussein Muyonjo, known across eastern Uganda simply as Swengere, wins a hotly contested parliamentary seat after years spent making people laugh on the radio, never once running for office before.

Three completely different people. Three completely different skills — engineering, music, comedy. And yet each of their stories runs through the exact same door: a political platform that did not exist a decade earlier, built by a man who had no way of knowing, at the time, whose lives it would eventually touch.

This book is not trying to prove a theological argument in the way a sermon would. It is simply laying three real stories side by side and asking the reader to notice what they share: influence built in one area of life — engineering competence, musical friendship, comic timability — becoming authority in a completely different area, once a door existed for it to pass through.

If there is a single thesis running underneath all three chapters, it is this: **you rarely see the door before you're already standing in it.** Ronald Balimwezo could not have planned for a citywide mayorship while serving one division. Nubian Li could not have known that encouraging a friend toward politics in 2017 would put him in office himself nine years later. Swengere could not have scripted a punchline that would end in a parliamentary seat.

None of them built their own door. All three walked through one anyway.
