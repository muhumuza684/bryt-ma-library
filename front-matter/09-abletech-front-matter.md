## BACK COVER COPY

The world tends to measure people by what they can't do. This book measures them by what they've already done — building businesses, creating art, solving problems, leading others — all while living with a disability the world assumed would disqualify them.

*AbleTech* is both a book and an idea in motion: a call to employers, churches, and governments to give disabled people the same chance everyone else already gets — and a preview of a company being built to prove, in practice, exactly what that chance can produce.

## INTRODUCTION

Somewhere along the way, the word "disabled" quietly became shorthand for "unable" — as if the absence of one ability erases every other ability a person carries. This book exists to correct that quiet, damaging shortcut.

The stories in these pages are not exaggerated, and they are not exceptions dressed up to look like the rule. They represent a pattern repeated everywhere, in every country, among people whose disability is the first thing the world notices and the last thing that actually defines what they're capable of. An entrepreneur who built a business despite what the world assumed she couldn't do. An artist whose limitation became the exact source of a style no one else could replicate. A leader whose disability taught him to solve problems differently than everyone around him — and better, because of it.

This book asks one direct question of its readers, and does not let them look away from it easily: **if a chance were actually given, not as charity, but as genuine opportunity, what would disabled people build?**

The second half of this book answers that question in a very literal way — by introducing AbleTech, a company built specifically to employ disabled people and let their work speak for itself, at scale, in public, where the results can't be dismissed as isolated exceptions. The book and the company share a name because they share a single purpose: not to ask the world for sympathy, but to show the world what it has been underestimating.

This is not a book about pity. It is a book about proof.

## APPENDIX A — A Note on the Case Studies
The case studies in this book are composite examples, built from real, common patterns rather than any single named individual's private story. This approach was chosen deliberately to protect the privacy and dignity of real disabled individuals, whose health and disability details are personal and were not obtained with consent for publication.

## APPENDIX B — About AbleTech (the Company)
AbleTech is a planned technology company built to employ only disabled people, intended as a living demonstration of this book's central argument. Details on the company's founding and progress will be added to future editions of this book as the company develops.

## APPENDIX C — A Call to Action
Employers, churches, and governments reading this book are invited to consider one concrete question: what is one opportunity, currently withheld from a disabled person in your own sphere of influence, that you could open this month?
