## BACK COVER COPY

I almost walked past them.

An old woman, carrying a child, on an ordinary road near Mbale. I had already made the decision to keep moving — until something in me refused to let it stand. I turned back. I said hello. And in that single moment of turning back, I saw something I have not been able to forget since: a bandage, covering a child's eyes.

This is a book about what happened after that moment — the friendship that followed, the diagnosis that broke my heart, and the hospital ward where I found more than thirty children living the same story. It is also a book of thanks — to the mission hospitals doing this work quietly, and to the ordinary people in my own life whose kindness I did not always know how to name until I sat down to write it here.

## INTRODUCTION

There is a particular kind of regret that comes from almost missing someone.

I was walking, that day near Mbale, with somewhere else to be. I saw an old woman ahead of me, carrying a child, and I made the small, unremarkable decision that most of us make dozens of times a day without noticing: I decided not to stop. I kept walking past them, already half-thinking about whatever came next.

And then something in me would not let that decision stand.

I do not have a clean explanation for what made me turn around. I only know that I did — and that turning around changed the next several months of my life, and the way I have thought about kindness ever since. When I finally greeted them, I saw what I had missed while walking past: a bandage, wrapped carefully over the child's eyes.

What began as a simple greeting became a friendship. What began as a friendship became a slow, painful education — about cancer, about the courage of a mother carrying a burden most of us will never have to carry, and eventually, about a hospital ward where this child's story turned out not to be rare at all. More than thirty children, in that same ward, living some version of the same reality.

I am not writing this book because I have a solution to offer. I am writing it because I saw something I cannot un-see, and because the place where I saw it — a mission hospital, doing this work with almost no fanfare — deserves far more support than it currently receives. If a single hospital can carry this much weight quietly, I keep asking myself what could happen if every church carried a portion of that weight too.

This book ends the way it should: with gratitude. Long before I ever met the woman and child on that road, other people had already been showing me, in smaller and quieter ways, exactly what it looks like to stop for someone rather than walk past them. This book is, in its final pages, my attempt to say their names out loud and thank them properly — something I do not think I did well enough while it was happening.

## APPENDIX A — Why Mission Hospitals
Mbarara Ruharo Mission Hospital, the model referenced throughout this book, represents a broader category of quiet, faith-driven medical care that receives far less recognition than it deserves. Readers who wish to support this work directly are encouraged to research mission hospitals operating in their own region.

## APPENDIX B — A Note on the People Named in This Book
The individuals thanked in this book's closing chapter are real people who showed the author genuine kindness over many years. Their stories are told here with gratitude, not for public commentary, but as a record — so that kindness rarely spoken of out loud does not go entirely unspoken forever.

## APPENDIX C — Reflection Questions
1. Who have you almost walked past — literally or figuratively — that you're glad you turned back for?
2. Is there a quiet act of kindness in your own life you've never properly thanked someone for?
