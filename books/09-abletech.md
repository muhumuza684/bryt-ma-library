# AbleTech

## Concept
A dual project: (1) a book about disabled people who serve or offer excellent services despite disability, meant to encourage the world to empower disabled people and give them the chance to have impact; and (2) a planned tech company that employs only disabled people, to show the world firsthand what they're capable of. The book and the company share the same name and brand.

## Naming History
Originally considered "Lame Tech" for both the book and the company. This was reconsidered and changed to **AbleTech**, because "lame" is an outdated and disrespectful term for disability in English (historically meaning "unable to walk," and now also slang for "bad/uncool") — using it risked undermining the project's actual empowerment goal, especially with international audiences where disability-rights language is closely watched.

## Locked Title
**AbleTech: What the World Calls Disabled, God Calls Capable**

Other subtitle directions considered:
- *AbleTech: Proof That Impact Has No Requirements*
- *AbleTech: The World Underestimated Them*

## Key Editorial Decision — Composite Examples, Not Real Named Individuals
The book's case studies will use invented/composite examples rather than real, named disabled people. Reasoning: a real person's disability is sensitive personal information, and using it in the book would require their explicit informed consent for both the disability disclosure and the life story details. Composite examples (illustrative, inspired by real patterns but not any single individual) avoid this ethical/legal complexity while keeping the message honest — many impact-driven books use this approach deliberately and disclose it upfront (e.g., "the following stories are illustrative, inspired by real patterns but not any single individual").

## Drafted Four-Part Structure

### Part 1 — The Lie We've Believed
Opens by naming the assumption the whole book pushes against: that disability equals inability. Sets up the emotional and moral stakes before any stories begin.

### Part 2 — Case Studies (the heart of the book)
Composite/invented people doing excellent work despite disability, spread across different fields: business, art, tech, sport, ministry. (To be drafted.)

### Part 3 — The Company as Proof
This is where AbleTech the company enters the book directly — not as a side project, but as the practical answer to everything the book has argued: "here's what happens when you actually give the chance the book has been asking for."

### Part 4 — The Call to Action
Directed at employers, churches, and governments — concrete, actionable asks, not just inspiration: what would it actually take for more "AbleTechs" to exist?
