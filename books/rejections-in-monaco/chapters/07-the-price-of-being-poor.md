# Chapter 7: The Price of Being Poor

The scholarship paid for things Eddie could not have paid for alone.

But there were still small expenses.

A better jacket.

A phone repair.

Food outside the basic routine.

A trip with classmates.

A gift for someone.

A train ticket.

The scholarship had changed his access to education. It had not turned him into a rich man.

Eddie watched classmates spend without thinking.

He learned to calculate.

Sometimes he stayed behind.

Sometimes he invented reasons.

Sometimes he simply said no.

At first, saying no made him feel poor.

Later it made him feel disciplined.

There was another kind of poverty too: the poverty of not knowing what people were talking about.

He had entered a world with customs he did not understand.

He learned by watching.

He learned which questions were rude.

Which questions were normal.

When to speak.

When to wait.

How to accept food.

How to refuse politely.

How to be a foreigner without apologising for existing.

> **Pull quote:** Being funded is not the same thing as feeling rich.
