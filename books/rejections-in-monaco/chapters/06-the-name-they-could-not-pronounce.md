# Chapter 6: The Name They Could Not Pronounce

The first time a lecturer asked Eddie a question in class, he knew the answer.

He also knew he might not be able to say it quickly enough.

Language had become a wall.

Not an impossible wall.

A slow wall.

Eddie began preparing before class. He wrote down terms. He listened to recordings. He repeated sounds until his mouth stopped fighting them.

His first academic embarrassment was ordinary.

He misunderstood an instruction.

He submitted something that did not answer the question the way the lecturer expected.

He received feedback.

For a few minutes he wanted to disappear.

Then he read the feedback again.

The paper had not rejected him as a person.

It had told him where he was weak.

That distinction changed him.

He stopped treating every correction as humiliation.

He started treating it as information.

The boy who had been rejected by Acheng had carried that rejection like a verdict.

China slowly taught him to carry failure like a map.

> **Pull quote:** Correction becomes useful when humiliation stops being the goal.
