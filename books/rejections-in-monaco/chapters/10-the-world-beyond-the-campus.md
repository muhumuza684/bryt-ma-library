# Chapter 10: The World Beyond the Campus

The first train journey that carried Eddie far from campus felt like a private education.

Outside the window, China kept changing.

Buildings gathered into cities.

Cities loosened into open land.

Stations appeared and disappeared.

People boarded with bags, children, food, phones, tired faces.

Eddie watched.

The world was not one thing.

China was not one picture.

It was millions of ordinary lives moving at once.

He began travelling when he could.

Sometimes the trip was carefully planned.

Sometimes he missed a connection.

Sometimes he got off at the wrong place and had to work out how to continue.

Those mistakes became stories.

He learned to navigate without panic.

He learned that being lost was often temporary.

He learned that strangers could help without knowing his history.

He also learned that homesickness could appear in beautiful places.

A crowded street could still make him miss Uganda.

A good meal could still remind him of home.

Travel did not erase where Eddie came from.

It made the map larger.

> **Pull quote:** A larger map does not erase the old home; it changes its size.
