# Chapter 8: A Door With No Handle

The door did not have a sign saying NO.

That was what made it difficult.

Eddie wanted an opportunity outside the classroom. He asked around. He sent messages. He introduced himself.

Some people answered.

Some did not.

One opportunity required experience he did not yet have.

Another depended on language.

Another depended on a network Eddie had not built.

He began noticing a pattern.

A closed door was not always an insult.

Sometimes the person on the other side simply did not know him.

So Eddie changed his strategy.

Instead of asking for the biggest opportunity, he asked for small responsibility.

Instead of saying he was capable, he tried to become useful.

He helped.

He showed up.

He returned messages.

He learned.

Trust came slowly.

It came without applause.

But when it arrived, Eddie understood something he had not understood in Mbarara.

Sometimes the key to a door is not a powerful person.

Sometimes it is a history of being reliable.

> **Pull quote:** Trust is often built through small tasks that nobody celebrates.
