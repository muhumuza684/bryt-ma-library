# When Agie Left

## Concept
A personal-story-driven nonfiction book centered on the moment the author's friend Agie disappeared for two years. The author admits he didn't fully respect their friendship while it was present — but learned to respect it in her absence. The book broadens this single relationship into a universal principle: **we don't respect what we have until its absence teaches us what it was worth** — and pushes a broader message to always respect what's at hand, since it could leave, using many other unique example-pairs (like peace vs. war) of things people take for granted that can be lost.

## Relationship to Other Projects
Thematically related to *Similar =/= Same* — both are built on contrast pairs. The distinction: *Similar =/= Same* studies things that LOOK alike but aren't; *When Agie Left* studies things that DISAPPEAR and only then reveal their true weight. Worth keeping as a separate, standalone book rather than folding into the other series.

## Drafted Three-Part Structure

### Part 1 — The Personal Story
Agie leaving, the two years of absence, and the author's own honest admission that he didn't fully value the friendship while it was there. Needs to stay personal and honest — readers trust the book more when the author admits his own blind spot first, rather than only pointing at other people's.

### Part 2 — The Pattern, Widened
Expands outward into "many unique examples" — contrast pairs where the first thing is invisible until the second thing (its absence/loss) reveals its value:
- **Peace vs. War** — peace is invisible until war shows what it cost to keep
- **Health vs. Sickness** — the body isn't thanked until it fails
- **Presence vs. Absence** — a parent, friend, or mentor undervalued until gone
- **Freedom vs. Captivity** — freedom feels ordinary until it's taken
- **Provision vs. Scarcity** — food, income, stability, invisible until missing

### Part 3 — The Return / Reflection
What it looked like when Agie came back (if she did), and what changed in how the author treated the friendship afterward — the redemptive turn. Not just "you'll lose things," but "here's what to do differently once you understand this."

## Status
Full three-part structure and initial pair bank for Part 2 are drafted. Not yet written in prose. Ending (whether/how Agie's return is portrayed) still to be developed with the author.
