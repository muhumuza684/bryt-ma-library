# Die to Live

## Concept
A faith-driven teaching book on the principle that a person must "die" first in order to truly live — dying to self, to flesh, to old identity. The author feels this principle is largely unknown to most people and wants to push it as widely as possible. Draws on biblical teaching (e.g., "kill the flesh first") as its foundation.

## Locked Four-Layer Structure (the book's spine)

### Layer 1 — Dying to Self/Flesh
The theological foundation. Paul's language of crucifying the flesh, being "born again." Answers WHY death has to come first at all: the old nature resists change by design and can't simply be improved — only put to death.

### Layer 2 — Dying to Old Identity
Widens the message beyond scripture-literate readers: old reputation, old mistakes, old relationships, the version of yourself people expect you to still be. This is where readers who aren't necessarily religious will still feel the book speaking directly to them.

### Layer 3 — Dying to Comfort/Fear
The practical, action-forcing part of the book — leaving what's safe (a job, a relationship, a familiar but limiting life) in order to grow. This is where the book stops being reflective and starts challenging the reader to actually do something.

### Layer 4 — Literal Death and Legacy (the Seed metaphor)
Closes the book by widening the lens once more — from personal transformation to legacy and what outlives a person. The seed metaphor (a seed must die/break open before it becomes a plant) works well here because it's both scriptural and a universally understood natural law.

## Positioning Note
Because this is built on a universal spiritual principle (not tied to any real named person or Uganda-specific context), it has genuine international reach potential — likely toward the global Christian/faith-reader market primarily, but the "old self vs. new self" framing also reaches general self-help readers who aren't religious.

## Guiding Caution (worth keeping in mind while writing)
"Push it as much as we can" should mean pursuing strong writing, clear structure, and smart positioning to maximize genuine reach — not chasing virality at the cost of substance. The strongest spiritual books earn their spread through resonance, not aggressive promotion.
