# Direction — Someone Better

Write this as a human book, not a motivational lecture. The central idea should be demonstrated through scenes, examples, comparisons, history, ordinary life, work, school, business, sport, technology, relationships, and leadership. The African leadership dimension is guidance for the author, not a target to attack or a political argument to make. Do not name or attack African leaders merely to make the point.

The recurring question is: what happens when a person finally accepts that they are not automatically the best?

Avoid turning the book into a ranking of people. 'Better' must always be contextual: someone can be better at one thing and weaker at another. The book should also distinguish healthy humility from inferiority, admiration from worship, and competition from cooperation.
