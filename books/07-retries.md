# Retries

## Concept
A multi-volume series about people who gave life multiple tries before they made it — in relationships and in life generally. Core message: life might be complicated, but the next try might be the one that works. Meant to give readers another reason to keep trying.

## Key Structural Decision
Originally, real celebrity couples were considered as examples (Rihanna & A$AP Rocky, Bill Gates's daughter Jennifer Gates & Nayel Nassar, Bobi Wine & Barbie). **This was reversed** — celebrities have been removed from the concept entirely, in favor of ordinary/anonymized people's stories, for several reasons:
- Avoids risk of inaccurate details about real people undermining the book's credibility (international fact-checkers scrutinize celebrity claims fast)
- Avoids political/relationship baggage tied to real public figures' private lives
- Opens the door to composite or anonymized examples that connect more honestly than celebrity gossip
- Fits the author's existing personal-witness voice already established in *Cries to Mbale* and *When Agie Left*

This positions "Retries" (along with Cries to Mbale, When Agie Left, and The Chokepoint) on the "personal-witness / anonymized" side of the author's growing catalog, as distinct from the celebrity-driven side (God of Balimwezo, She Da Moon).

## Recommended Structural Split (proposed, not yet finalized)
Rather than blending relationship persistence and life/career persistence into one book, split into two lanes under the same "Retries" umbrella brand:
- **Retries: Love Edition** — stories of people who broke up, drifted, or took years before a relationship finally worked
- **Retries: Life Edition** — stories of people who failed at a career, business, or dream multiple times before breaking through (larger international market — startup failure stories, late bloomers, "no after no after yes")

## What Makes It Shareable Internationally (per discussion)
- Each chapter should end with a single quotable, standalone line built for social sharing (e.g., "She said no. He asked again. It's called practice, not desperation.") — this is what spreads on TikTok/Instagram/Reels before anyone reads the book itself.
- Recommended chapter rhythm: a relatable example → a universal principle → a direct challenge to the reader ("what have you stopped trying?"). Alternating like this avoids reading as either pure gossip or generic self-help.
- Title should stay simple and translate cleanly: *Retries* alone is short and strong; suggested pairing subtitle: *"Retries: Why the Best Things in Life Come on the Second Try."*

## Status
Concept, celebrity-removal decision, and international-structuring guidance are locked. The Love Edition / Life Edition split has been proposed but not yet confirmed by the author. No composite story examples have been drafted yet. Not yet written in prose.
