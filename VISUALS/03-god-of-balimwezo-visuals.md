# VISUALS — God of Balimwezo Series
### Illustration Briefs for Cartoon / Anime-Style Chapter Art

## Why Stylized Illustration Instead of Real Photos
This series is about real, living public figures, but the book itself avoids taking political sides or reproducing copyrighted photographs (screenshots, news photos, and social media images all belong to whoever captured them). Turning key real moments into stylized cartoon or anime-style illustrations solves both problems: no copyright issue, and no risk of a real photo being read as political commentary. Each brief below describes the SCENE and the FEELING, not a specific photo to copy — hand these to an illustrator or an AI image generator as a starting prompt, and adjust art style/wording to taste.

**Recommended consistent art style across the whole series:** warm, semi-realistic African cartoon/anime style — think editorial illustration rather than exaggerated comic-book style. Warm color palette (golds, reds, deep blues) tying into Uganda's flag colors where appropriate. Consistent character designs for Balimwezo, Bobi Wine, Ssenyonyi, Nubian Li, and Swengere across all three volumes, so readers recognize each figure book to book.

**A note on faces:** for a book like this, it often reads better — and stays safely non-political — if character illustrations are stylized enough to evoke the person (build, posture, signature clothing/colors) without being a precise likeness. This keeps the tone warm and universal rather than tabloid-like.

## VOLUME 1 — God of Balimwezo

### Illustration 1 (Chapter 1 opener) — "The Unnoticed Years"
*Scene:* A man in simple work clothes, hard hat under one arm, standing at the edge of a modest local council building, quietly reviewing papers. Ordinary streets around him, mid-day light, nothing dramatic happening.
*Feeling:* Faithfulness without applause. No crowd, no fanfare — just steady, unnoticed work.

### Illustration 2 (Chapter 2 opener) — "A Door Being Built Elsewhere"
*Scene:* Split illustration — on one side, a musician on a stage under bright lights; on the other side, the same figure in plain clothes walking alone toward a modest building, guitar case set down, taking off a stage jacket. A literal door stands between the two halves of the image, slightly ajar, light spilling through.
*Feeling:* A costly, quiet decision being made, far from anyone who will eventually benefit from it.

### Illustration 3 (Chapter 4, "The Door Opens") — Inspired by the real kneeling moment, reimagined respectfully
*Scene:* Two stylized figures facing each other warmly outdoors, one bowing slightly in a gesture of respect and gratitude, the other reaching down with an open hand to help him back up — mutual respect shown through posture, not likeness. Soft golden light, a crowd blurred and out of focus in the background.
*Feeling:* Humility and gratitude, not hierarchy. This should read as two people honoring each other, not one person subordinate to another.

### Illustration 4 (Chapter 4, ceremonial) — "The Robe and the Chain"
*Scene:* A stylized ceremonial robe (deep red, gold trim, echoing a mayoral chain of office) being placed over a man's shoulders by unseen hands, in a grand hall filled with soft, celebratory light. Face can remain partially obscured/turned, focus on the robe and the moment.
*Feeling:* Arrival — the door fully opened, the years of unseen preparation now visible to everyone.

## VOLUME 2 — The Door Bobi Wine Opened

### Illustration 5 (Chapter opener) — "Two Roads, One Door"
*Scene:* A single doorway seen from behind, warm light spilling out of it. On one side of the frame, a figure in an engineer's hard hat approaches from a construction site; on the other, a figure with a microphone and notepad approaches from a radio studio. Both walking toward the same door from very different directions.
*Feeling:* Two very different starting points, converging on the same opportunity.

### Illustration 6 (Chapter, Ssenyonyi's welcome) — "Welcomed, Not Waited On"
*Scene:* A warm illustration of one stylized figure in a suit stepping out of a vehicle, being greeted by another figure with an open hand and a genuine smile, other well-wishers blurred in the background. Parliament-style building softly rendered behind them.
*Feeling:* Warmth, welcome, mutual respect — deliberately avoids any comparison to another figure, stands alone as its own moment.

### Illustration 7 (Chapter, closing) — "Two Names, One Door"
*Scene:* Two stylized figures standing side by side, facing forward toward the reader, a single glowing doorway behind them large enough for both. Neither figure positioned as more important than the other.
*Feeling:* Parallel paths, equal dignity, the same door serving two different callings.

## VOLUME 3 — Kingmaker

### Illustration 8 (Chapter opener) — "Three Trades, One Door"
*Scene:* Three small vignettes arranged in a triptych: a blueprint and drafting tools (engineering), a guitar and a microphone (music), a comedy stage spotlight and a radio mic (comedy) — all three vignettes rendered with light lines converging toward a single glowing doorway in the center of the image.
*Feeling:* Different skills, same principle, same door.

### Illustration 9 (Nubian Li chapter, based on the real swearing-in) — "Sworn In"
*Scene:* A stylized figure in ceremonial mayoral robes, one hand raised, a religious text (rendered generically, not a specific identifiable copy) held by an official beside him under an umbrella, a crowd of well-wishers blurred behind. Warm midday light, celebratory but dignified tone.
*Feeling:* A friend's encouragement, years earlier, quietly arriving at this exact moment.

### Illustration 10 (Nubian Li chapter, the two-way door) — "The Door That Swung Back"
*Scene:* A double-exposure style illustration — faded in the background, two young friends sitting together talking under a tree, one gesturing persuasively; in the foreground, sharper and in full color, the same figure years later in ceremonial robes being sworn in.
*Feeling:* Time passing, the same relationship producing two different doors, years apart.

### Illustration 11 (Swengere chapter, based on the real swearing-in) — "The Comedian Takes the Oath"
*Scene:* A stylized figure in a formal suit, one hand raised, a small holy book held beside him, a slightly wry, warm half-smile still visible even in the formal moment — a visual nod to his comedic background without undermining the dignity of the scene. Parliament-style backdrop, red and gold color accents.
*Feeling:* The same warmth that made people laugh for years, now standing in service of something larger.

### Illustration 12 (closing chapter) — "The Pattern"
*Scene:* An overhead, almost map-like illustration — a single glowing door at the center, with three distinct paths radiating outward to three small vignette figures (engineer, singer, comedian), each connected back to the door by a thin line of light.
*Feeling:* The whole book's thesis in one image — different people, one door, one pattern.

## Images Deliberately NOT Recommended for This Series
- **Any direct comparison between two real political figures** (e.g., a "who got the better chair" contrast) — even stylized, this reads as political commentary and breaks the series' neutrality commitment. Leave this kind of comparison out entirely.
- **Any image built to closely mimic a specific viral photo or screenshot** — even in cartoon form, a close visual match to a specific real photo can still carry the same political charge the original had. Keep illustrations impressionistic, not recreations.
- **Direct, identifiable facial likenesses of any of the four real figures** — stylize enough that the illustration evokes the person through posture, setting, and story context rather than a precise portrait.

## How to Use These Briefs
Each brief is written to be handed directly to an illustrator or pasted into an AI image generation tool, with the art style note at the top applied consistently. Generate a small style-test batch first (2–3 images) to lock in a consistent character/color style before producing the full set, so all illustrations across all three volumes feel like they belong to the same book.
